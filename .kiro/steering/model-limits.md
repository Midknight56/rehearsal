# Model limits

What Rehearsal's model does not simulate, and how every output is labelled. Rules for `src/ui/**`, `src/replay/**`, `src/comparison/**`, `src/exports/**`, `README.md`, `docs/**`, the write-up, the video script and the blog post. They implement sections 1, 2, 3, 6, 7 and 8 of `plans/rehearsal-implementation-plan.md` (revision 2, 2026-11-03); the claims policy in `claims-policy.md` applies on top of them.

## What the model is
- One station is one service stage: a single FIFO line feeding identical parallel servers. A cohort follows its ordered station IDs, each at most once, then exits. Staffing is constant through a run. Queue capacity is unlimited and nobody leaves early. Walking is at one constant speed along the computed grid path. Service durations are constant or triangular. These sentences appear in the assumptions panel beside the tables and in the fixed footer of every printable export, in that wording.
- The output of a run is a scenario result under those assumptions for one seed and one replication. It is not a forecast, a prediction, a crowd-density model, a capacity recommendation or a safety finding, and no screen, export, README, video, blog post or write-up presents it as one. The phrase on screen and in the video is `scenario results, not a forecast`.

## What it does not simulate (say so next to the results)
- No personal space, congestion-dependent walking speed, doorway throughput, accessibility clearance, crowd pressure or collisions: queue sprites never block movement. The assumptions panel states this, and the legend under the game view reads `Line colour shows simulated waiting, not measured crowd density.`
- No real crowd physics, collision avoidance, emergency evacuation, safety certification, weather, abandonment (nobody leaves a line), staff breaks, scheduled staffing changes, venue capacity recommendations, live ticketing, automatic optimal layouts, AI-generated forecasts or spreadsheet formula evaluation (a formula cell is read by its saved value and marked `formula (saved value used)`; one with no saved value is a field error).
- The limits are part of the model and are stated where results are shown: at most 5,000 attendees, 10 stations, 6 station visits per route, 80 × 80 cells, a 360-minute observation window, servers 1 to 20, 10 saved scenarios, a 2 MiB import file.
- The Harbor Hall Community Fair, its 120 attendees, the 8/15/22 s and 6/10/14 s service assumptions and every number derived from them are fictional and illustrative. Never describe them as measured, observed or typical.

## Labels that every output carries
- Every result, export and printable carries the scenario hash, `modelVersion`, `baseSeed`, the replication, `horizonMs` and `completion`. The replay panel is headed `Game view` with the subtitle `Replay of replication 0, one example run` (the selected replication replaces 0). One replayed replication is an example, never the average of the 30 paired runs.
- Paired comparison reports the mean paired change and the empirical 10th to 90th percentile range of the 30 paired differences, labelled exactly `variation across these simulated runs`, with N shown. Never `confidence interval`, `prediction interval`, `margin of error` or `significant`. Improvement counts such as `candidate had lower mean wait in 27/30 runs` come only from the actual results.
- When the admitted populations differ between baseline and candidate (any cohort count or ID set changes), highlight the change and disable person-for-person improvement language. Never state or test that adding staff improves every downstream metric; a change list says what changed, the metric cards say what the model produced.
- When `completion` is `drain-capped`, every completion-conditioned metric shows its `conditioned` mark and the note `from <n> of <admitted> admitted attendees; <k> unfinished at the drain cap`; a shorter mean from completed people is never shown as an improvement for everyone. Undefined averages and percentiles show `n/a`, never 0.
- The words `optimized`, `optimised`, `optimal`, `safe`, `certified` and `recommended` do not appear as whole words in the source string literals of `src/ui/print/**` or `src/ui/results/**` (`tests/ui/vocabulary.test.ts`) or in the generated exports (one list, the one of `paired-comparison-and-release` requirement 8.4, which that spec's next review aligns to this line); `forecast` appears only inside the fixed phrase `not a forecast`, which lives in `src/exports/assumptions-text.ts` and which the scan excludes; no badge, icon or colour anywhere suggests approval, safety, risk or density. A heat-coloured queue line means simulated waiting only.
- Results whose snapshot hash differs from the current scenario are labelled `Stale, rerun` on every result surface and stay readable until the rerun. The run sheet needs no run: it lists staffing, stations and walking, and carries the label only on its results line.
- The landing page says `Everything runs in this browser.`; the sentence `Everything runs in this browser; no account and nothing uploaded.` appears only after the network inspection of matrix row 21 is recorded on the deployed URL (a static host still receives ordinary page requests, and the README says so).
- Performance figures (first example within 3 s, a 500-attendee run below 500 ms, 30 paired 500-attendee runs within 5 s, 60 or 30 frames per second) are targets until `docs/performance.md` records them with the named laptop, browser and date. Accessibility is a WCAG 2.2 AA target; conformance is not claimed without an audit. If no organizer walkthrough happened, the README and write-up say the product was not user-tested, and no demand or time-saving claim is made.
- The write-up claims an inspectable workflow for one narrow audience, not novelty over existing simulation or layout tools. The pitch line is `A spreadsheet, a floor plan and a game engine, stitched into an event rehearsal.`; the categories it names are the three parts, not a claim about what the model predicts.
